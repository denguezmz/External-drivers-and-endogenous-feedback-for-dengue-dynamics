An Integrative Mechanism–Behaviour–Data Modelling Framework for Dengue Dynamics and Early Warning

Abstract
Although existing dengue models typically focus on short-term temporal scales and climatic drivers, climate factors alone cannot fully explain complex multi-year transmission dynamics. 
This study demonstrates that behavioural feedback, mediated by risk perception, constitutes a pivotal determinant in shaping complex epidemic trajectories. 
We developed a hybrid modelling framework integrating mechanistic dynamics with neural networks to systematically quantify behavioural feedback through the dual lenses of contact heterogeneity and mosquito biting rates. 
Validated against four years of longitudinal data from Guangdong, this framework reveals a biphasic regulatory mechanism wherein behavioural feedback amplifies transmission during the early outbreak but suppresses it in later stages.
Using symbolic regression, we derived two explicit analytical expressions for the behavioural feedback function.
Furthermore, the framework’s generalizability was validated across multiple diverse regions, enabling a comparative analysis of behavioural heterogeneities between different areas. 
Finally, we propose a risk-warning model to forecast dengue outbreak levels, serving as a practical complement to the mechanistic approach. 
This work establishes an integrative mechanistic-data-driven analytical paradigm and provides two universal functional forms for behavioural feedback, offering a robust framework for investigating vector-borne disease dynamics.
